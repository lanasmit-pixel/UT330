First Two Weeks of UT330

September 11, 2026 · Weeks 1–2

The first two weeks of UT330 have honestly been really fun. I have never really enjoyed coding before this class, but I think a big reason is that we actually get to use our own ideas and creativity. Instead of just writing code because we are told exactly what it needs to do, we have had the freedom to make things feel like our own.

Building my own website

One of the first things we did was build our own MySpace-style website. This was probably my favorite assignment so far because we got to decide what we wanted the website to look like and actually play around with the code. I was able to experiment with colors, fonts, layouts, images, and other small details until the website started to feel like me.

I have realized that I enjoy coding a lot more when I can connect it to something I am actually interested in. Having the freedom to make design decisions made coding feel less like a technical assignment and more like creating something.

Looking at everyday spaces differently

Another assignment we worked on was taking pictures of what it is like to move through a physical space and thinking about it through Interaction Design. This helped me realize that interaction design is not just about websites, apps, and screens. The spaces we use every day are also designed to tell us where to go, what to do, and what is allowed.

My Biggby observation

For this assignment, I went to Biggby and ordered a drink while paying attention to how the space worked for someone using it for the first time. I noticed a few things that made the experience more confusing than it needed to be.

  • The doors were not very accessible or easy to use.
  • The ordering area was not clearly labeled.
  • The layout could be confusing for someone who had never been there before.
  • A shield in the ordering area made it impossible to scan coupons.

What I found interesting was that none of these things were necessarily huge problems on their own. But together, they changed how easy it was to move through the space and understand what I was supposed to do. It made me start thinking about how the same thing happens with websites and apps.

What I have learned so far

The biggest thing I have learned in the first two weeks is that design is everywhere. I am starting to pay more attention to the little things that tell people where to go, what to click, what to touch, and what to do next. I also learned that coding can actually be creative, which is something I did not expect to enjoy as much as I do.

I am excited to keep learning how physical spaces, websites, and technology all connect to the way people experience the world around them.
